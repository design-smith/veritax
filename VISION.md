# Veritax can grow into a $20B company by following one compounding path

> **Own the data tax professionals already pay heavily for → use that data product to acquire the profession → observe what work they perform with the data → build specialized tools that perform that work → expand the data required by those tools → connect those mature tools through an intelligent spine → become the execution layer for corporate tax.**

The sequence matters because every stage finances and teaches the next one.

## 2026: Where Veritax starts

Today, Veritax begins with a very concrete asset: a global company database built specifically for tax professionals.

There are roughly **32,854 public companies** spanning the major public markets of North America, South America, Europe, Asia, Africa and Oceania. The source systems behind those markets are known. Remaining work is closing population gaps and, critically, separating **data access from redistribution rights**.

Every fact in Veritax should eventually know:

```text
Company
Field
Value
Original source
Permitted source
Retrieved date
Freshness
License status
Redistributable?
Confidence
Alternative sources
```

The database therefore evolves from a table of companies into a **provenance-aware company knowledge system**.

For fields coming from sources with restrictive redistribution terms, Veritax systematically finds permissible alternative provenance: government registries, regulatory filings, GLEIF, issuer disclosures, open government sources, tax authorities and other legitimate sources.

The objective is a global dataset Veritax can commercially expose with confidence.

The product is designed from day one around a specific initial professional: **the transfer-pricing practitioner.**

## Stage 1: Build the best company-data product for TP professionals (now through 2027)

The first product should become extraordinarily good at one simple promise:

> **Everything a TP professional needs to understand and compare companies, in one place, already organized the way they work.**

The $90 seat gets them through the door.

A practitioner who previously had access to expensive databases should be able to open Veritax and rapidly find companies, legal identity, parent/subsidiary relationships, geographic footprint, industries, operating descriptions, financial statements and ratios, historical financials, exchanges and identifiers, ownership, subsidiaries, acquisitions, business activity, filings, potentially comparable companies, jurisdiction, and supporting sources.

Having the fields is insufficient. **Presentation itself should understand transfer pricing.** Financials should appear in the orientation practitioners expect. Company screening should behave like a TP benchmarking study.

A user should be able to begin with ~30,000 companies and see a visible funnel:

```text
32,854 global companies
      ↓
8,421 relevant jurisdictions
      ↓
1,936 appropriate industries
      ↓
412 ownership criteria satisfied
      ↓
78 financial criteria satisfied
      ↓
24 candidate comparables
```

Every screening criterion should remain visible. Search strategies should be saveable. A practitioner should be able to take an old Big Four benchmarking study and reproduce its methodology inside Veritax.

The first goal is **trust through replication**. Once they reproduce existing work, they begin performing new work directly in Veritax.
